Global Business Model Canvas

One canvas, three dimensions: economic, societal, environmental.

The Global Business Model Canvas (GBMC) is a one-page business model framework that maps how an organisation creates, delivers and captures value, and reads that value in full. Global as in whole, not as in worldwide: every key block is examined for what it means for the economy of the business, for society, and for the environment.

Created by Martial Bombrault, Academic Director at IRIIG, Lyon

What it changes

The canvas keeps the familiar logic of the Business Model Canvas by Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur, so anyone who knows it can start at once. Five shifts make it global.

Customer Segments

Beneficiaries

Value reaches more people than those who pay. Users, communities and the wider environment count as beneficiaries too.

Key Partners

Partners & Ecosystems

A business depends on networks, territories and natural systems, not only on contracted partners.

Key Activities

Key Processes

The focus moves from separate tasks to the chains of activity that produce value from start to finish.

One layer

Three layers

Partners, value propositions and beneficiaries are each read on economic, societal and environmental levels.

Costs vs revenues

A three-part balance

Societal and environmental costs sit beside economic ones, each set against what the business gives back.

Reading the balance

The lower half of the canvas is where the model shows its full result. Each row sets what the business takes against what it gives, on one dimension at a time. A model can be profitable while running a deficit with society or the environment, and the canvas makes that visible at a glance.

Questions

What is the Global Business Model Canvas?

A one-page tool created by Martial Bombrault for designing and analysing business models across three dimensions at once: economic, societal and environmental.

Why is it called global?

Global here means whole, not worldwide. The canvas looks at the full value a business creates and consumes. It is not a tool for international expansion.

How is it different from the Business Model Canvas?

It keeps the logic of Osterwalder and Pigneur's canvas, but splits partners, value propositions and beneficiaries into three layers, replaces customers with beneficiaries, and sets three kinds of costs against revenues and contributions to society and the environment.

How is it different from the Triple Layered Business Model Canvas?

The Triple Layered Business Model Canvas by Joyce and Paquin (2016) uses three separate canvases, one per dimension. The Global Business Model Canvas keeps all three on a single sheet, so the trade-offs between them are visible side by side.

Who is it for?

Students, entrepreneurs, managers and consultants who want to design or review a business model with its societal and environmental effects in view from the start.
